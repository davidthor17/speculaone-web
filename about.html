The Person Behind Specula

Built from the inside of luxury hospitality.

D.T. Founder, Specula

Where it began

My career in hospitality began on the front line.

At a five-star property in Iceland, I started as a Host, welcoming guests, guiding them through the property, and becoming deeply familiar with every part of the experience.

The role taught me that luxury hospitality is rarely about simply delivering what is requested. It is about understanding what a guest wants before they have to ask, handling demanding requests calmly, and turning expectations into experiences guests remember.

I eventually progressed into a Butler role, entrusted with the personal experience of high-net-worth and high-profile guests, becoming their primary point of contact and, in many cases, the face of the property during their stay.

That experience became the foundation for Specula.

Why Specula exists

I saw first-hand the difference between what a hotel believes it delivers and what a guest actually experiences. I also learned that the details that matter most are often the ones that never appear on a standard inspection sheet.

The way a guest is welcomed when nobody knows they are being observed. The way a difficult request is handled. The small problem that is solved before it becomes a complaint. The difference between service that is technically correct and service that feels genuinely exceptional.

That is where Specula began.

Specula was created to experience hotels exactly as their guests do, without advance notice, without preferential treatment, and without the property knowing that an audit is taking place.

We make the booking. We arrive as guests. We experience the property as guests. And we document what happens.

The audit is conducted anonymously. The property does not know when the audit is taking place, who the auditor is, or that their guest experience is being evaluated.

The hotel sees a guest. Specula sees the experience.

The purpose is not to find fault, and it is not to sell a consultancy report. It is to answer one simple question:

What does this hotel actually feel like to a guest?

Because the most revealing hotel inspection is the one the hotel doesn't know is happening.
